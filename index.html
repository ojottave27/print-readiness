Are your files ready to print?

Send me your STLs and I return, file by file, a verdict with the measured value and the exact fix. Not an opinion: every line of the report is a measurement.

The problem

A failed print costs filament, machine hours and, above all, the deadline you promised the client. In most cases the cause is already in the file: inverted normals, walls too thin, loose fragments, an open surface. The slicer accepts the file and the problem only shows up ten hours later.

The report answers the questions a print brief asks, with a number behind each one.

When something fails, the report says what it means and what to do. Example:

Normals failed Inverted or inconsistent normals between faces.
Fix: recalculate normals outward (Blender: Shift+N with everything selected).
Scale failed Measured height 0.20 mm against 20 mm expected.
Fix: check the export units (mm vs cm vs inches).
One rule that matters to the client: when a check cannot run, the report says “did not run” and never assumes it passed. A file with missing checks never shows up as “ready”.

Two services

1. Readiness report

You send a folder of STLs. You get the verdict per file, the measured values and the fix for every failure. Use it to launch a product, accept third-party work, or sign off a batch before it reaches the machine.

2. Clearance calibration

You print a hole-and-pin coupon that I supply, measure it and send me the numbers. I then hand you the parts with the clearance corrected for your machine — because your printer does not err the same way as the one next door.

What this service does not do

I say it before you start: I do not repair the mesh automatically, I do not do hollowing, I do not generate supports and I do not quote printing jobs. I diagnose precisely and tell you exactly what to fix — we fix it together, or I show you how in two minutes in Blender. I would rather lose a sale than promise something that does not exist yet.

How to start

Send me one file, ideally one that has already given you trouble. I return the report at no cost. If the diagnosis is useful to you, we agree on the rest.
